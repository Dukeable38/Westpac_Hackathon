Catching invoice scams before the money leaves

A plain-English brief for the team: what we're building, why it matters, and what Lauren and Nathan can do over the next day. No IT background needed.

The problem, as a story

Mia runs a small landscaping business. Every month she pays her mulch supplier about $3,000. One Tuesday an email arrives from the supplier, same logo, same invoice layout, saying "We've changed banks, please use our new account details below."

She pays. The email didn't come from her supplier. A scammer had broken into the supplier's email, or made a near-identical address, and changed only the bank details. The money lands in a scammer's account and is moved on within hours. Mia's real supplier still wants paying.

This is called business email compromise or invoice redirection. Westpac says it makes up 94% of the scam money reported by its institutional banking customers.

Our idea in three sentences

  1. Before paying an invoice in Westpac's online banking, the customer can upload the invoice PDF.
  2. An AI assistant checks it three ways: does the invoice make sense on its own, does it match what the bank already knows about this supplier, and does the outside world (business register, websites) back it up?
  3. It gives a confidence score with reasons, and the customer can chat with it to explain things it couldn't know, like "yes, they're a brand-new business".
Why banks would want this: banks usually learn about a scam only after the money is gone, and many scams are never reported. Our tool sees invoices before payment, both genuine and fake. Over time that builds the data banks need to recognise scams earlier.
How it works

The journey of one invoice

Think of it like medical testing

A cheap screening test runs on everyone. Only if it flags something do you order the expensive, specific tests. We do the same: quick checks on every invoice, deeper online research only when something looks off. That keeps it fast for the 95% of invoices that are fine.

The "fingerprint" idea

Any file can be turned into a unique code. Change one comma and the code changes completely. We store the code with the payment, so months later you can prove which exact invoice you paid against, even if someone has edited their copy since.

Words you'll hear

Mini glossary

TermWhat it means
BSB + account numberThe classic way to identify an Australian bank account. The BSB (6 digits) identifies the bank and branch.
PayIDA shortcut for an account: pay a phone number, email or ABN instead of typing a BSB.
BPAYPaying bills with a biller code and reference number, common for utilities and big suppliers.

Business email compromise (BEC)A scammer gets into, or imitates, a real business's email and sends invoices with their own bank details.
Mule accountA bank account scammers use to receive stolen money and pass it on, often opened in someone else's name.
OSINT"Open-source intelligence": researching using only public information such as registers, websites and social media.
Hash / fingerprintThe unique code generated from a file, described on the previous page.
Confidence scoreOur 0–100 rating of how confident we are the invoice and payee are genuine, always shown with reasons.
Scope

What we cover, and what to ignore

Please keep research inside the green box. If you find a great stat about something in the grey box, it doesn't help our pitch, so skip it.

In scope

  • Businesses and sole traders paying supplier invoices
  • Invoice redirection (real supplier, swapped bank details)
  • Fake invoices from businesses that don't exist
  • Fake bills pretending to be a real business or the ATO
  • Invoices edited after payment, and disputes about them
  • Payments by BSB, PayID or BPAY

Out of scope

  • Credit and debit card fraud, card skimming
  • Romance, investment, crypto and job scams
  • Hacked online-banking logins, stolen passwords
  • Phishing texts asking for codes or passwords
  • Personal payments between friends and family

Questions to answerDue: 9:30am Tuesday

Good places to look: Scamwatch "Targeting Scams" report (scamwatch.gov.au), ASD/ACSC Annual Cyber Threat Report (cyber.gov.au), ASIC, AFP, Australian Banking Association, Westpac newsroom, ABC News. Prefer 2024–2026 figures.
